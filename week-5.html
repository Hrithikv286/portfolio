Week 5 — UI/UX, Survey, Project Management & Technical Research

Week 5 progressed from UI/UX design into user research, project management principles, a startup client meeting, and engineering calculations. The week helped me understand how a product is shaped not only by technical design, but also by the needs of its users, the way the project is planned and managed, and the requirements of a real client.

Session 1 — UI/UX Design & User Research

In the first session, we were introduced to the core principles of UI/UX design using Figma. We learned how user interface (UI) design focuses on the look and layout of screens, such as colours, typography, buttons, and visual hierarchy, while user experience (UX) design focuses on how easy, clear, and satisfying a product is to use. We explored how to plan screen layouts, organise information logically, and design interfaces that are simple and intuitive for the user. During this session, we also received the formal problem statement for our project. To understand the problem from the user's point of view, we prepared and conducted user surveys and collected responses from potential users. Based on the survey findings, we analysed the users' needs, expectations, and difficulties, and used these insights to refine the problem requirements for our digital interface. This activity taught me that good design starts with understanding the user, and that decisions should be based on real feedback rather than assumptions.

Session 2 — Project Management Workshop (Bosch Expert)

In the second session, we attended a dedicated Project Management workshop conducted by Bhuvan Sundar Surappaiya from Bosch. The session introduced us to the fundamentals of project planning, including how to define the scope and objectives of a project, break the work down into manageable tasks, and set clear milestones to track progress. We also learned about execution frameworks used in industry to organise and carry out projects, and about resource tracking, which involves managing time, people, budget, and materials so that a project stays on schedule. Learning from an industry expert gave me practical insight into how engineering projects are managed in a professional environment and showed me the importance of planning, coordination, and communication in completing a project successfully.

Client Meeting & Technical Research (Startup Elaicle)

Later in the week, I participated in a technical client meeting with representatives from the startup Elaicle. The purpose of the meeting was to clarify the system requirements and the operational scope of the project, so that our team clearly understood what the client expected from the solution. This was a valuable experience in communicating with a real client, asking the right questions, and translating the client's needs into technical requirements.

Following the meeting, I carried out technical research related to the project. I studied battery sizing calculations, learning how to select the battery voltage and calculate the required ampere-hour (Ah) capacity based on the system's power requirements and expected operating time. I also studied motor sizing calculations, focusing on how to determine the load torque and match the motor's speed (RPM) to the needs of the application. In addition, I completed a recommended technical course suggested by the founder of Elaicle, which strengthened my understanding of the concepts required for the project. Finally, I researched the working principles of hub motors and explored the solutions currently available in the market, which helped me understand how hub motors are used in practical applications and what options exist for our project.

Overall, Week 5 was a valuable learning experience that combined design thinking, management skills, and engineering analysis. Through UI/UX design and user surveys, I learned the importance of designing around the needs of the user. The project management workshop gave me an industry perspective on planning and executing projects, while the client meeting with Elaicle and the technical research on battery sizing, motor sizing, and hub motors strengthened my ability to understand real requirements and apply engineering calculations to a practical problem.
